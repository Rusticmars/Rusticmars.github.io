Every material, from the silicon in a computer chip to the nitride crystals proposed for next-generation memory, owes its behavior to the quantum mechanics of its electrons. This site presents research that predicts that behavior from the atoms alone, spanning magnetism, light, heat, and electric polarization, and explains in plain language what the results mean and why they matter.

Research, in plain language

Research is grouped by theme. Each theme opens to its topics, each with a short explanation of the question being asked and why it matters. The common thread is a single idea: given only the atoms in a material, quantum theory can predict how that material will behave.

The doctoral topics (magnetism in graphene, light guiding along nanoparticle chains, and spin transport) are the subject of the dissertation Energy Dispersion, Magnetism, and Transport in Three Condensed Matter Systems (The Ohio State University, 2015; advisor Prof. David Stroud).
